# Barcelona Atlas visual identity

The app and browser icon use the supplied logo unchanged (`public/static/barcelona-atlas-logo.png`). The interface extends its navy, blue, yellow and ivory palette with a restrained heraldic red.

| Colour | Hex | Purpose and inspiration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Navy | `#0b3062` | Logo outlines, primary text and controls; our maritime interpretation. |
| Mediterranean blue | `#197bea` | Active borders, focus rings and map accents; drawn from the logo. |
| Gold | `#ffd23f` | Navigation accents and warm heatmap steps; echoes the gold field of the four bars. |
| Sant Jordi red | `#b8323e` | Errors and traffic intensity; echoes the cross and bars in the city's heraldry. |
| Warm ivory | `#fffdf7` | Paper-like interface surfaces, echoing the logo background. |

Barcelona's heraldry combines the cross of Sant Jordi with four red bars on gold. The city explains these symbols in [El Joc del Corpus](https://www.barcelona.cat/culturapopular/sites/default/files/retallables8.pdf). These hex values are design choices, not official municipal specifications. Blue's Mediterranean association is our interpretation.

The logo's flower recalls Barcelona's iconic panot paving. According to [betevé's history of the panot](https://beteve.cat/va-passar-aqui/panot-paviment-iconic-barcelona/), Fontanella became the first street fully paved with the flower design in 1908. We make no attribution of the flower's designer.

Gold is an accent, not body text on white. Navy and slate provide readable labels. Map layers retain distinct sequential or categorical palettes, including purple and teal where needed to distinguish datasets; colour intensity remains relative within each layer. This palette does not introduce a shared quantitative scale across datasets.
